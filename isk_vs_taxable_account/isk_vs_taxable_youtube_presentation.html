01 · The tax rules

Two accounts. Two tax clocks.

ISK — tax on account value

Tax each year on a base calculated from quarter values and deposits.

In 2026: 1.065% of the capital base above your shared SEK 300,000 allowance.

That tax is due even in a loss year. Sales inside the ISK do not trigger capital-gains tax.

The SEK 300,000 allowance is shared across ISK, capital insurance and PEPP.

Ordinary account — tax when income is recognized

30% tax on dividends and realized gains.

Fund units also pay 0.12% yearly on their value at the start of the year.

Unrealized gains can wait until sale. Loss relief depends on your tax situation.

A listed fund may qualify for the optional 20% cost-basis method when it has risen more than 400%.

Sources: Skatteverket, ISK tax and 2026 allowance; fund-unit and capital-gains rules. Full links on the final slide.
Use the phrase “tax clocks.” ISK charges a recurring tax tied to account value. A normal account defers much of the tax until a sale, but fund units have a small annual deemed-income tax. Do not say ordinary account is tax-free until sale: dividends and the annual fund tax still apply.

03 · The ordinary account

Tax deferral has value. So does tax on returns.

30%

When you sell at a gain

The tax is normally on the realized gain, not the full sale price. Deferring a sale leaves more money invested for longer.

0.12%

Every year for fund units

0.4% is treated as income and taxed at 30%, based on fund value at the start of the year.

20%

Optional cost basis

For eligible listed fund units, cost basis can be set to 20% of the sale proceeds. This can help when the fund’s value has more than quintupled (grown by more than 400%) since purchase.

A loss may be deductible. This presentation models a simplified 21% value for losses; actual tax relief depends on offsetting income and deficit rules.
Sources: Skatteverket fund rules, cost-basis method and capital-loss rules. Individual tax outcomes can differ.
A useful YouTube point: “30% of a gain” is not 30% of the whole account. Also note the standard cost basis method becomes more favorable when the investment has risen more than 400%. Our model compares actual average cost against the 20% method at sale.

08 · Moving abroad

What if you plan to leave Sweden?

A move changes your tax residence. Swedish rules may continue to matter, and your destination’s rules depend on where you go.

Sweden · after departure

Close ties can mean you remain fully tax liable and report worldwide income after moving.

If you become limited tax liable, Swedish ISK standard-rate income and gains from fund-unit sales generally are not declared.

The 10-year rule covers certain shares, not fund units. Some deferred share-exchange gains can be taxed when leaving the EEA.

Your new country · after arrival

Check how it taxes fund income, distributions and gains when you eventually sell.

Some countries also tax investment wealth or treat accumulating funds differently.

Ask your broker whether you can keep the account after becoming a non-resident.

The next slide models the years up to your move under Swedish rules; it keeps ordinary-account gains unrealized at the move date.
Sources: Skatteverket on moving, close ties and capital income while living abroad. Links on the final slide.
Say: “There is no single tax result for everyone who leaves Sweden. First determine whether Sweden still treats you as fully taxable. If you become limited tax liable, Swedish ISK standard-rate income and fund-unit gains generally are not declared, though specific share rules and deferred share-exchange gains matter. Then check the tax rules and provider terms in the country where you will live.”

10 · Takeaways

The answer changes with the return path

In this SWDA sample, ISK usually finished higher. Under low or negative returns, the ordinary account can win.

Think about expected return.
ISK charges tax on the account base even when the investment falls.
Think about the selling date.
An ordinary account can defer gain tax; the plan for withdrawals matters.
Think about the 2026 allowance.
The SEK 300,000 benefit is shared across ISK, KF and PEPP.
Think about existing unrealized gains.
Transferring a holding into ISK can trigger a taxable sale.
Model boundaries: one accumulating global equity fund; 2026 future tax settings held constant; full allowance assumed available; transaction costs and individualized loss offsets excluded. Cross-border taxes are not modeled. Not personal tax advice.
End with: “For new global-equity savings under these assumptions, ISK looked strong in the historical replay. That does not make it the universal winner. If returns are weak, the allowance is already used, you already have large unrealized gains, or you move abroad, check your own tax position.”
